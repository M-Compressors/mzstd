# lzbench run, 2026-11-12

mzstd 0.2.14 (see the [changelog](../../CHANGELOG.md): the batched
sequence encoder, the `fast` finder's search loop and match-length count,
the decoder's sequence-loop write prefetch and its lockstep Huffman loops)
and the previous release, 0.2.13, against zstd 1.5.7, measured with the
lzbench in-memory benchmark on the Silesia corpus. The README's lzbench
table is taken from the 0.2.14 rounds.

## Environment

- lzbench master at
  [`a6fa8aa`](https://github.com/inikep/lzbench/tree/a6fa8aa758932a02841c0cc68c07e5abf8870951)
  (2026-11-07), reporting itself as lzbench 2.4.1. It vendors zstd 1.5.7
  and mzstd 0.2.11. For each build its `lz+entropy/mzstd` directory was
  replaced by the sources under test: this release's (the working tree
  on `2c15c38` with the 0.2.14 changes, built before its version number
  changed from 0.2.13), and commit `14cf1c1`, whose library sources are
  those of the v0.2.13 tag (the tag changes only the version number, the
  homepage and the documentation). lzbench still labels the codec "mzstd
  0.2.11", the version its codec table names, so the output below is
  relabelled.
- gcc 15.2.0, rustc 1.99.1. Built with
  `make BUILD_ONLY='ZSTD MZSTD' MOREFLAGS="-march=native"`; the Rust codecs
  use lzbench's default `RUST_TARGET_CPU=native`, so both sides target the
  same CPU. One lzbench binary serves both builds: it loads the Rust
  codecs from a shared library, and each build's library is selected with
  `LD_LIBRARY_PATH`.
- AMD Ryzen 9 7950X VDS (2 vCPU, KVM), Linux 7.0, default CPU governor and
  boost. The host is shared with other tenants. The benchmark is pinned to
  one vCPU with `taskset -c 1`.
- Input: `silesia.tar`, 211,957,760 bytes, the twelve Silesia files in a
  ustar archive, compressed as one stream.

## Command

```sh
taskset -c 1 ./lzbench -t8,8 -o1 \
  -ezstd_fast,-5/mzstd_fast,-5/zstd_fast,-3/mzstd_fast,-3/zstd_fast,-1/mzstd_fast,-1/zstd,1/mzstd,1/zstd,2/mzstd,2/zstd,3/mzstd,3/zstd,5/mzstd,5/zstd,8/mzstd,8/zstd,11/mzstd,11/zstd,15/mzstd,15/zstd,18/mzstd,18/zstd,22/mzstd,22 \
  silesia.tar
```

`-t8,8` repeats each compression and decompression for at least eight seconds
and reports the fastest iteration. Codecs are interleaved level by level so
that drift on the shared host affects both sides of a pair alike. Because
other tenants load the host, the command ran in rounds, each the 0.2.14
build followed by the 0.2.13 build (round 1 from 19:36 to
20:00 UTC, rounds 2 to 4 from 20:00 to 21:11 UTC). The tables below take,
for every codec and level, the fastest result of rounds 2 to 4, extending
lzbench's own fastest-iteration rule. Round 1 is left out: the benchmark
charts were being regenerated on the guest's other vCPU during its
0.2.14 run, which slowed both codecs at the first levels. The raw
output of every round, round 1 included, is at the end.

Decompression ratios of 0.2.14 reproduce within about 1.5%
between rounds at the `--fast` levels and at levels 1, 5 and 8; round 2
measured levels 2 and 3 three to six percent below the other two rounds.
Compression reproduces within about 5% at the `--fast` levels and levels
1 to 3, and moves by up to 33 points between rounds from level 5 up
(level 5: 0.966 to 1.190, level 11: 0.861 to 1.189) on this host, so
differences of a few percent there are within noise.

Against 0.2.13 in the same rounds, relative to zstd:

- Decompression at the `--fast` levels went from 0.92-0.93 to zstd's speed
  (0.929 to 1.006, 0.924 to 0.998 and 0.931 to 0.987 at `--fast` 5, 3 and
  1), from the sequence loop's write prefetch, and gained 1.2-1.7 points at
  levels 1 to 3 (0.939 to 0.956, 0.935 to 0.947, 0.940 to 0.956), from the
  lockstep Huffman loops. Levels 5 to 15 are within a point of 0.2.13.
  Levels 18 and 22 measure 0.966 and 0.936 against 0.939 and 0.895, where
  0.2.13's rounds spread from 0.798 to 0.949.
- Compression at the `--fast` levels went from 0.956-0.958 to 0.993-1.002,
  and levels 1, 2, 3 and 8 gained 1.6 to 3.7 points (0.963 to 0.979, 0.952
  to 0.989, 0.989 to 1.011, 0.962 to 0.990), from the batched sequence
  encoder and, up to level 2, the `fast` finder. Level 5 measures 0.976
  against 1.031 and levels 11 to 22 measure 1.049-1.247 against
  0.984-1.167; their finders are unchanged and both differences are
  inside the spread of the rounds.

What remains behind zstd: compression by 0.4-2.4% at `--fast` 5 and 1 and
at levels 1, 2, 5 and 8. Decompression is level with zstd at `--fast` 5
and 3 (1.006 and 0.998), 1.3% behind at `--fast` 1 and 3.4-6.4% behind
from level 1 up.
